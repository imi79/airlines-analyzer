For the three sample PDFs (M7). Each fits the PDF's eight-line box. Every figure is a verified
figure from the tearsheet; figures still awaiting review (Air France-KLM's undrawn facilities

## Air France-KLM, FY2025

Improving credit from a still-leveraged base. Higher EBITDA (EUR 5.1bn, margin up 2.2 points to
15.3%) took net leverage down to 1.71x and interest coverage up to 8.06x, even as net debt rose
to EUR 8.7bn.
Free cash flow turned positive at EUR 606m but stays thin against the fleet programme: FCF to
debt of 3.9% is the weakest of the network peers. Cash covers 20.8% of revenue, also the lowest
of the group, and advance ticket sales equal 62.2% of it.
The 2025 refinancing (EMTN bonds, hybrid redemption, credit facility extended) supports the
maturity profile. Further deleveraging needs EBITDA to hold above EUR 5bn while capex stays high.

## easyJet, FY2025 (Oct 2024 to Sep 2025)

The strongest balance sheet among the low-cost peers: net cash of GBP 602m, gross leverage of
2.03x and FFO to debt of 44.0%, all first in the group. Free cash flow of GBP 624m covers 21.3%
of debt, and liquidity of GBP 4.8bn (47.4% of revenue) includes the new GBP 1.3bn undrawn
revolving facility. Moody's and S&P investment grade ratings were affirmed, with stable outlooks.
Profitability is the constraint: the EBITDA margin of 14.2% is the lowest of the group, against a
median of 21.2%, and unit revenue (7.52 pence) runs only slightly ahead of unit cost (7.00 pence).
Coverage slipped to 9.22x as financing costs rose.

## Aena, FY2025

A strong, regulated infrastructure credit. The EBITDA margin of 60.3% is the highest of the
airport group, net leverage is 1.46x and interest coverage 14.72x. Moody's upgraded the rating
and Fitch affirmed it with a stable outlook; the regulator set next year's average charge per
passenger above this year's.
The investment cycle is the point to watch: capex intensity rose 2.6 points to 16.9% and free
cash flow fell 10.2% to EUR 1.7bn, though FCF to debt of 22.9% is still the best in the group.
Liquidity of EUR 4.3bn (68.4% of revenue, including EUR 2.2bn undrawn) covers the programme
comfortably.
