# International Airlines Group, FY2024

Fact sheet `9b7da2f3a001`. Sections marked **draft-v8** were redrafted in M8; the others keep their draft-v7 text (M4).

## Snapshot (draft-v7, passed)

1. International Airlines Group, the owner of British Airways, Iberia, Aer Lingus and Vueling, ended FY2024 with a stronger credit profile, and both Moody's and S&P upgraded the group to investment grade during the year.
2. Net leverage fell by 0.53x to 1.13x as EBITDA rose by 19.3% to EUR 6.6bn on revenue up 9.0%.
3. The point to watch is unit cost: CASK ex-fuel rose by 3.4% to 5.89 euro cents, while undrawn committed facilities fell by 22.9% to EUR 3.4bn.

## Credit metrics (draft-v7, passed)

1. EBITDA increased by 19.3% to EUR 6.6bn, and operating profit rose by 22.1% to EUR 4.3bn even as depreciation, amortisation and impairment grew by 14.6% to EUR 2.4bn.
2. Total debt rose by 7.9% to EUR 17.3bn, but net debt was reduced by 18.7% to EUR 7.5bn, taking gross leverage down by 0.28x to 2.61x.
3. Interest coverage improved by 3.16x to 8.70x as finance costs fell by 17.6% to EUR 917m.
4. Cash generation strengthened: FFO rose by 31.9% to EUR 5.6bn, lifting FFO to debt by 5.9 points to 32.5%, and free cash flow increased by 169.4% to EUR 3.6bn as capex fell by 14.2% to EUR 2.8bn.

## Operating performance (draft-v7, passed)

1. Capacity grew by 6.2% to 343.3bn available seat kilometres, carrying 122.0m passengers.
2. Unit revenue outpaced unit cost: RASK rose by 2.6% to 9.35 euro cents, while CASK was broadly unchanged at 8.10 euro cents.
3. CASK ex-fuel rose by 3.4% to 5.89 euro cents, and the fuel share of costs fell by 1.7 points to 27.4% with fuel cost broadly stable at EUR 7.6bn.
4. The fleet grew by 3.3% to 601 aircraft, with an average age of 12.4 years, and reliability issues with Rolls-Royce Trent and Pratt and Whitney GTF engines continued to affect operations.

## Liquidity and funding (draft-v8, passed)

1. Liquidity rose by 17.6% to EUR 13.2bn, of which EUR 3.4bn in undrawn committed facilities, and liquidity to revenue reached 41.2%.
2. Debt due within one year rose by 16.7% to EUR 3.5bn, but short-term debt cover was stable at 3.81x, so the near-term maturities are covered by liquidity without new funding.
3. Free cash flow after lease payments rose by 259.2% to EUR 2.4bn, lifting FCF after leases to debt by 9.7 points to 13.9%, the comparable measure of cash left for debt holders once lease payments in financing cash flows are taken out.
4. Customer prepayments remain large, with contract liabilities of EUR 8.3bn, up 7.3%, and the group replaced its secured revolving credit facility with a new sustainability-linked facility during the year.

## Peer positioning (draft-v7, passed)

1. IAG leads the network airlines on EBITDA margin (1st of 3), net leverage (1st of 3) and FFO to debt (1st of 3).
2. Interest coverage of 8.70x is above the peer median of 6.51x, and cash liquidity to revenue of 30.6% is the highest of the group (1st of 3).
3. The weakest position is advance ticket cover, at 84.8% against a peer median of 74.5% (3rd of 3), while capex intensity sits in the middle of the group.

## Watch items (draft-v8, passed)

1. Unit cost: CASK ex-fuel rose by 3.4% to 5.89 euro cents, so non-fuel costs are absorbing part of the revenue gain.
2. Undrawn facilities: committed undrawn lines fell by 22.9% to EUR 3.4bn, a thinner buffer alongside liquidity of EUR 13.2bn.
3. Customer prepayments: advance ticket cover of 84.8% is above the peer median of 74.5% but the weakest of the group (3rd of 3), leaving the cash position sensitive to a slower booking cycle.
4. Fleet and operations: the average fleet age rose by 3.3% to 12.4 years, and reliability issues with Rolls-Royce Trent and Pratt and Whitney GTF engines continued to affect operations.
