# Norwegian Air Shuttle, FY2025

Fact sheet `c5ce0b4ceedf`. Sections marked **draft-v8** were redrafted in M8; the others keep their draft-v7 text (M4).

## Snapshot (draft-v7, passed)

1. Norwegian Air Shuttle, the Norwegian low-cost carrier, grew revenue by 6.6% to NOK 37.6bn in FY2025, with the EBITDA margin widening by 2.3 points to 21.2%.
2. Net leverage rose by 0.41x to 1.19x as net debt increased by 82.6% to NOK 9.5bn, in line with the peer median.
3. The point to watch is cash liquidity, which fell by 4.1 points to 26.7% of revenue, below the peer median.

## Credit metrics (draft-v7, passed)

1. EBITDA increased by 19.2% to NOK 8.0bn, and operating profit rose by 99.3% to NOK 3.7bn as depreciation, amortisation and impairment fell by 12.0% to NOK 4.2bn.
2. Total debt rose by 21.6% to NOK 19.6bn, so gross leverage was broadly unchanged at 2.46x while net leverage climbed to 1.19x.
3. Interest coverage improved to 6.27x even as finance costs rose by 12.0% to NOK 1.3bn.
4. FFO rose by 21.1% to NOK 6.9bn, leaving FFO to debt stable at 35.4%, but free cash flow fell by 49.9% to NOK 2.9bn as capex rose by 299.8% to NOK 5.0bn.
5. FCF to debt fell by 20.8 points to 14.6%, in line with the peer median, with capex intensity up by 9.7 points to 13.2%.

## Operating performance (draft-v7, passed)

1. Passengers rose by 3.4% to 27.3m, supported by a summer programme that added new leisure routes.
2. Fuel cost increased by 2.1% to NOK 9.6bn, and fuel made up 28.2% of costs, broadly unchanged on the prior year and in line with the peer median.
3. Cash from operations rose by 12.9% to NOK 7.8bn, while capital spending on aircraft and intangibles absorbed more of that cash.
4. The acquisition of previously leased Boeing 737-800 aircraft and the exercise of options on additional 737 MAX 8 aircraft underpin the fleet programme.

## Liquidity and funding (draft-v8, passed)

1. Cash and cash-like investments fell by 7.5% to NOK 10.1bn, taking cash liquidity to 26.7% of revenue, with undrawn committed facilities not part of that measure.
2. Debt due within one year was broadly unchanged at NOK 3.8bn, so cash and cash-like investments of NOK 10.1bn cover the near-term maturities without new funding.
3. Free cash flow after lease payments fell by 80.6% to NOK 603m, or 3.1% of debt, down 16.2 points, as net capex rose by 302.2% to NOK 4.5bn.
4. Customer prepayments rose, with contract liabilities up 6.1% to NOK 4.4bn and advance ticket cover at 43.7%, up 5.6 points.

## Peer positioning (draft-v7, passed)

1. Norwegian sits in the middle of the low-cost group on EBITDA margin, net leverage, gross leverage, interest coverage, FFO to debt and FCF to debt, ranking 2nd of 3 on net leverage.
2. Its strongest metric is advance ticket cover, the lowest of the group (1st of 3), against a peer median of 55.1%.
3. Cash liquidity to revenue is the weakest of the group (3rd of 3), against a peer median of 34.3%, while capex intensity is above the peer median of 9.9%.

## Watch items (draft-v8, passed)

1. Cash liquidity: cash and cash-like investments fell by 7.5% to NOK 10.1bn, leaving cash liquidity to revenue at 26.7%, the weakest of the low-cost group (3rd of 3).
2. Cash flow after leases: free cash flow after lease payments fell by 80.6% to NOK 603m, only 3.1% of debt, as net capex rose by 302.2%.
3. Leverage: net leverage rose by 0.41x to 1.19x as net debt increased by 82.6% to NOK 9.5bn, in line with the peer median.
4. Investment: capex intensity rose by 9.7 points to 13.2%, above the peer median of 9.9%, with options exercised on additional 737 MAX 8 aircraft.
