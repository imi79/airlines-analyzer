# Norwegian Air Shuttle, FY2024

Fact sheet `80fd00463f3c`. Sections marked **draft-v8** were redrafted in M8; the others keep their draft-v7 text (M4).

## Snapshot (draft-v7, passed)

1. Norwegian Air Shuttle, the Norwegian low-cost carrier, grew revenue by 38.3% to NOK 35.3bn in FY2024, a year in which the acquisition of Widerøe was finalised and delivery delays from Boeing constrained fleet growth.
2. The EBITDA margin narrowed by 3.7 points to 18.9%, while net leverage was broadly unchanged at 0.78x.
3. The point to watch is cash liquidity, which fell by 6.3 points to 30.8% of revenue, the weakest of the low-cost peer group (3rd of 3).

## Credit metrics (draft-v7, passed)

1. EBITDA rose by 15.9% to NOK 6.7bn, but operating profit fell by 16.1% to NOK 1.9bn as depreciation, amortisation and impairment increased by 36.0% to NOK 4.8bn.
2. Total debt increased by 14.6% to NOK 16.1bn and net debt by 14.3% to NOK 5.2bn, leaving gross leverage broadly unchanged at 2.41x.
3. Interest coverage weakened to 5.89x as finance costs rose by 33.8% to NOK 1.1bn.
4. Cash generation improved: FFO rose by 11.1% to NOK 5.7bn and free cash flow by 20.9% to NOK 5.7bn, although FFO to debt eased by 1.1 points to 35.6% as total debt increased by 14.6%.
5. FCF to debt improved by 1.8 points to 35.4%, with capex intensity rising by 1.4 points to 3.5% as capital spending increased by 129.9% to NOK 1.2bn.

## Operating performance (draft-v7, passed)

1. Passengers rose by 28.2% to 26.4m, supported by the Widerøe acquisition completed at the start of the year.
2. Fuel cost increased by 21.5% to NOK 9.4bn, while the fuel share of costs fell by 5.1 points to 28.0% as revenue rose by 38.3%.
3. The operating result was held back by the weakening of the Norwegian krone against the US dollar and by external disruptions during the year, with operating profit down 16.1% to NOK 1.9bn.
4. Collective bargaining agreements concluded during the year raised personnel cost across several markets while reducing the risk of industrial action.

## Liquidity and funding (draft-v8, passed)

1. Cash and cash-like investments rose by 14.8% to NOK 10.9bn, equal to cash liquidity to revenue of 30.8%, which covers the debt due within one year of NOK 3.8bn without new funding.
2. Debt due within one year increased by 123.5% to NOK 3.8bn, so the refinancing task is larger than a year earlier even though cash liquidity exceeds it.
3. Free cash flow after lease payments rose by 17.5% to NOK 3.1bn, equal to 19.3% of debt, broadly unchanged on the prior year.
4. Customer prepayments rose with contract liabilities up 29.4% to NOK 4.1bn, and advance ticket cover increased by 4.3 points to 38.1%.

## Peer positioning (draft-v7, passed)

1. Norwegian has the lowest gross leverage of the low-cost group (1st of 3) and the strongest FCF to debt (1st of 3), against a peer median of 16.3%.
2. Cash liquidity to revenue is the weakest of the group (3rd of 3), against a peer median of 33.0%.
3. On EBITDA margin, net leverage, interest coverage and FFO to debt the company sits in the middle of the group, and its advance ticket cover is the lowest of the peers (1st of 3).

## Watch items (draft-v8, passed)

1. Cash liquidity: cash liquidity to revenue fell by 6.3 points to 30.8%, below the peer median of 33.0% and the weakest of the group (3rd of 3).
2. Maturities: debt due within one year rose by 123.5% to NOK 3.8bn, which has to be met from cash and cash-like investments of NOK 10.9bn and from cash flow after leases of NOK 3.1bn.
3. Margins: the EBITDA margin narrowed by 3.7 points to 18.9%, and interest coverage eased by 0.91x to 5.89x as finance costs increased by 33.8% to NOK 1.1bn.
4. Shareholder returns: the Board intends to propose a profit distribution per share for the year, subject to approval from debt instrument holders.
