# International Airlines Group, FY2025

Fact sheet `e9efd4675603`. Sections marked **draft-v8** were redrafted in M8; the others keep their draft-v7 text (M4).

## Snapshot (draft-v7, passed)

1. International Airlines Group, the owner of British Airways, Iberia and Aer Lingus, grew revenue by 3.5% to EUR 33.2bn in FY2025, a year in which industrial action and supply-chain constraints disrupted operations.
2. The EBITDA margin widened by 2.3 points to 23.0% and net leverage fell by 0.35x to 0.78x, the lowest of the network peers (1st of 3).
3. The point to watch is liquidity: cash liquidity to revenue fell by 5.6 points to 25.0% as cash was returned to shareholders.

## Credit metrics (draft-v7, passed)

1. EBITDA increased by 15.1% to EUR 7.7bn, and operating profit rose by 17.3% to EUR 5.0bn even as depreciation, amortisation and impairment grew by 11.2%.
2. Total debt was reduced by 17.7% to EUR 14.3bn and net debt by 20.9% to EUR 5.9bn, taking gross leverage down by 0.75x to 1.86x.
3. Interest coverage improved by 2.79x to 11.49x as finance costs fell by 6.1% to EUR 861m.
4. FFO rose by 15.3% to EUR 6.5bn, lifting FFO to debt by 13.0 points to 45.5%.
5. Free cash flow fell by 11.5% to EUR 3.1bn as capex rose by 22.2% to EUR 3.4bn, although FCF to debt still improved by 1.6 points to 22.1% on the smaller debt base.

## Operating performance (draft-v7, passed)

1. Capacity grew by 2.4% to 351.4bn available seat kilometres, while traffic was broadly unchanged, with RPK stable at 300.8bn and passengers stable at 121.6m.
2. Unit revenue improved: RASK rose by 1.1% to 9.45 euro cents and passenger yield by 1.1% to 9.63 euro cents.
3. CASK fell by 1.0% to 8.02 euro cents, but CASK ex-fuel rose by 2.0% to 6.01 euro cents as fuel cost declined by 6.9% to EUR 7.1bn and its share of costs eased by 2.3 points to 25.1%.
4. The fleet grew by 4.3% to 627 aircraft at an average age of 12.6 years, broadly unchanged, with engine availability constraining British Airways' Middle East schedule.

## Liquidity and funding (draft-v8, passed)

1. Liquidity fell by 17.2% to EUR 10.9bn, of which EUR 2.6bn in undrawn committed facilities, down 22.7%, and cash and cash-like investments of EUR 8.3bn.
2. Debt due within one year was reduced by 12.3% to EUR 3.0bn, covered 3.59x by liquidity, and borrowings due within two years of EUR 4.1bn are covered 2.68x by liquidity, so near-term maturities are met without new funding.
3. Free cash flow after lease payments, the comparable measure of cash left for debt holders, fell by 23.9% to EUR 1.8bn, and FCF after leases to debt declined by 1.1 points to 12.8% as net capex rose by 53.0% to EUR 3.4bn.
4. Advance ticket cover rose by 11.8 points to 96.6%, while contract liabilities of EUR 8.0bn were reduced by 3.6%, so a larger share of the cash base is customers' prepayments.

## Peer positioning (draft-v7, passed)

1. IAG leads the network airlines on leverage and cash generation, with the lowest net leverage (1st of 3) and gross leverage (1st of 3) and the highest FFO to debt (1st of 3).
2. Its EBITDA margin is the highest of the group (1st of 3), well above the peer median of 15.3%, and interest coverage also ranks at the top (1st of 3).
3. The weakest point is customer prepayment cover, where advance ticket cover is the highest of the group (3rd of 3) against a peer median of 67.4%, while capex intensity and cash liquidity to revenue sit in the middle of the group.

## Watch items (draft-v8, passed)

1. Liquidity: liquidity to revenue fell by 8.2 points to 33.0% and cash liquidity to revenue by 5.6 points to 25.0%, in line with the peer median, as cash was returned to shareholders.
2. Cash flow after leases: FCF after leases fell by 23.9% to EUR 1.8bn and FCF after leases to debt by 1.1 points to 12.8%, as net capex rose by 53.0%.
3. Customer prepayments: advance ticket cover of 96.6% is the highest of the network peers (3rd of 3) and above the peer median of 67.4%, a sensitivity if bookings slow.
4. Near-term maturities: short-term debt cover eased by 0.22x to 3.59x as liquidity fell by 17.2% to EUR 10.9bn.
