## Aena, FY2023

**Snapshot**

Aena, the Spanish airport operator, had a stronger FY2023 as traffic recovered: passengers rose by 16.2% to 283.2m.
The EBITDA margin widened by 10.3 points to 60.0%, the highest of the airport peers (1st of 3).
Net leverage fell to 2.06x even as capex intensity rose to 27.4%, the main point to watch as the investment plan unfolds.

**Credit metrics**

EBITDA increased by 45.4% to EUR 3.0bn on revenue of EUR 5.0bn, up 20.5%.
Net debt was stable at EUR 6.2bn, so the earnings recovery brought net leverage down by 0.94x and gross leverage to 2.84x.
FFO to debt improved to 27.7%, but free cash flow declined by 26.3% to EUR 837m on heavier capital spending.
Interest coverage weakened to 12.61x from a very high 22.34x, still in line with the peer median.

**Operating performance**

Passengers at the Spanish network rose by 16.2% to 283.2m, although traffic growth slowed from the rebound of the year before, to 16.2%.
Revenue per passenger increased by [[revenue_per_passenger.change@FY2023?]] to [[revenue_per_passenger@FY2023?]], with aeronautical revenue per passenger at EUR 9.77.
Non-aeronautical activities made up [[non_aero_share@FY2023?]] of revenue.

**Liquidity and funding**

Liquidity increased by 33.7% to EUR 4.9bn, including EUR 2.6bn of undrawn committed facilities.
Cash and cash-like investments rose to EUR 2.4bn, and liquidity to revenue reached 97.6%, a comfortable buffer against near-term maturities.

**Peer positioning**

Aena has the highest EBITDA margin among the airport peers (1st of 3), at 60.0% against a median of 42.2%.
Leverage sits at the group median, with net leverage of 2.06x ranking [[net_leverage.peer_rank@FY2023?]].
Cash liquidity to revenue is the strongest of the group ([[cash_liquidity_to_revenue.peer_rank@FY2023?]]).

**Watch items**

- Investment: capex intensity rose by 10.0 points to 27.4%, above the peer median of 15.9%.
- Free cash flow: FCF to debt fell to 9.7% as capital spending absorbed more of the cash generated.
- Regulation: airport charges are set by the regulator, so revenue per passenger depends on the next tariff decision.

## International Airlines Group, FY2023

**Snapshot**

International Airlines Group ended FY2023 with a stronger credit profile, as the earnings recovery brought net leverage down to 1.66x from 3.10x.
The EBITDA margin rose by 4.4 points to 18.9%, the best in the network peer group (1st of 3).
Liquidity is the point to watch: cash and cash-like investments fell by 28.8% as total debt was reduced by 19.5%, taking liquidity to revenue down to 38.2%.

**Credit metrics**

EBITDA increased by 66.4% to EUR 5.6bn, on revenue up 27.7% at EUR 29.5bn.
Gross leverage fell to 2.89x from 5.97x, and net debt was reduced by 11.0% to EUR 9.2bn.
Cash generation strengthened: FFO to debt rose to 26.6% and FCF to debt to 8.2%.
Interest coverage improved to 5.54x, while capex intensity eased to 11.1%.

**Operating performance**

Capacity grew by 22.6% to 323.1bn available seat kilometres.
Unit revenue outpaced unit cost: RASK rose by 4.2% to 9.12 euro cents, while CASK fell by 2.9% to 8.03 euro cents.
CASK ex-fuel declined by 4.3%, and the fuel share of costs edged up to 29.1%.
The fleet stood at 582 aircraft, with an average age of 12.0 years.

**Liquidity and funding**

Liquidity fell by 12.7% to EUR 11.2bn, of which EUR 4.4bn in undrawn committed facilities.
Undrawn facilities increased by 34.3%, partly offsetting lower cash and cash-like investments of EUR 6.8bn.
Advance ticket cover rose to 113.6%, the highest in the peer group (3rd of 3), so a larger part of the cash is customers' prepayments.

**Peer positioning**

IAG leads the network airlines on EBITDA margin (1st of 3), FFO to debt (1st of 3) and FCF to debt (1st of 3).
Gross leverage is below the network median of 3.27x, while net leverage sits in the middle of the group.
Cash liquidity to revenue is the weakest of the group (3rd of 3).

**Watch items**

- Liquidity: liquidity to revenue fell by 17.7 points to 38.2% as cash was used to repay debt.
- Customer prepayments: advance ticket cover of 113.6% is above the peer median of 55.0%, a sensitivity if bookings slow.
- Fuel: fuel makes up 29.1% of costs, so margins remain exposed to jet fuel prices.
